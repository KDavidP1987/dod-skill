# DoD: calibration — dry-run evidence before approval, the miss history beside coverage, rework beside the rate, the build host in the profile

**Size:** L — one script gains four rules and two printed lines (`skills/dod/scripts/dod-index.mjs`: a new Log-note grammar and an approval rule, a store-wide miss history, an amendment field, a profile section), four reference documents and the skill's README change, and every store is affected at once for plans approved after the rule's date. No schema, no auth, no new external dependency; the M test fails on the number of modules and on the rule reaching every store.
**Planned:** interactively — four decisions put to the owner on 2026-09-26 in plan mode (page https://claude.ai/artifact/JhJ4Z1Z6b6abwsAjqKRNYT, the account of that session), every recommendation taken: a dry run is a problem at approval (S-1), the miss history is computed from the store plus the profile's project probes (S-2), rework is a `reworks:` field (S-3), the host is a `## Host` profile section and a recon rule, with no further check (S-4). The scope before those four answers was fixed by the root-cause page https://claude.ai/artifact/GbVuMRNsTdAk8FpDi5iDS8 (remedies 1–3 and the rework line; remedy 5 not chosen).
**Request:** epic `dod-v0-2` A18: "calibration (findings 1 and 10: evidence depth on probes with a miss history and the historical miss rate beside the coverage line; per plan, review findings that fell on probes with a profile note)" and A20: "(1) dry-run evidence — before `approve`, every `cmd:` that can run on the current tree and every checker the plan names for its own landing are run against the draft and their output recorded; (2) amendment twins — an amendment names every other place in the plan stating the same thing and the input that makes its new control fail, and `--check` warns when it names neither; (3) platform spikes — recon measures each external contract a D-item depends on with a one-line spike on the build host, cited in the plan, and profile.md records the host platform; (4) a `Rework  <r> of <m> misses corrected an earlier amendment` line beside the prediction rate in the report, never replacing it".

## Purpose & typical use

1.1 — Who uses it: the author of every dod plan (an agent, most often) and the owner who approves it, once per plan at approval and once at close. The sentence they would say: "show me what this plan's commands actually print before I approve it" and "how often has this kind of probe been missed here before".
1.2 — The job: make approval rest on observed output rather than stated expectation, and make the report separate plan foresight from amendment quality. The measured cause is `docs/calibration/miss-analysis-2026-09-26.md`: of 117 misses, 30 were sets stated from memory, 26 were rework of an amendment, 22 were contracts assumed rather than measured, and all three are visible before approval if the commands are run on the draft.
1.3 — What exists today: the rate (`reportNumbers`, `dod-index.mjs` line 988), the index's `Most-missed layers` line (`renderIndex`, line 1419), the profile's `## Audience` section (the only one the script reads, lines 1502–1560) and a free-text `## Project probes` section nobody parses. This child extends all four; it replaces none.

## Use cases

### Typical
An author finishes a draft of `event-watch`. Before asking for review they run each `cmd` item's command on today's tree and write what it printed as a dry-run note — `paths-walked` says two paths are undeclared, so they fix the plan now instead of amending it at build step 11. `--check event-watch` shows `coverage 15/15 layers · 49/49 probes · miss history: 7.2 in 4 of 9 done plans · …`, so the owner sees which answers deserve a harder look. At close, the report says `Rework 1 of 3 misses corrected an earlier amendment`.

### Minimal stretch
8.1 — A plan with no `test` or `cmd` item needs no dry-run note (D2 asks only those types); a store with no done plan has an empty history and prints `none yet` rather than a number (D5, D8); a profile with no `## Host` or `## Project probes` section is valid (D3, D11). A plan approved before 2026-10-02 is never asked anything new (D2, D9).
8.2 — Used once: the notes stay in the plan's Log as its record; nothing else is written, no file lingers (D20 walks the paths, D21 removes the clone).

### Maximal stretch
9.1 — Volume: expected is this store's 17 plans and about 120 counted amendments; 100× is 1,700 plans and 12,000 amendments. The history adds one pass over amendments the index already parses, linear in their count; the budget is 2,000 ms for 1,000 done plans of 20 amendments each (`calib.scale`, D3), and beyond it only time grows — no bound refuses, and memory is one entry per distinct probe, at most 49. The history line lists at most 49 probes, wrapped at 120 columns (D5, D8). A plan of 200 items needs at most 200 notes, one per test or cmd item — which is the point.
9.2 — Misuse: an author who records `n/a` for every item passes D2 but not D4 on any history probe (D4), and every `n/a` reason is visible to the reviewer; a note that pastes a home path is warned (D24); `reworks:` pointing forward or at itself is a problem (D6).
9.3 — Repeated: two dry-run notes for one item are both kept and both count; running `--check` twice prints the same lines (D1, D22).

## Business rules

4.1 — Thresholds: a probe enters the history at two done plans (S-2); an n/a reason is at least 12 characters, the same bound as a Coverage `prose:` reason (D1); the rules apply from 2026-10-02, compared as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings against `baselined` (D2, D4) and against the amendment's date (D9). The thresholds are stated once, in `MESSAGES_CALIBRATION`'s neighbour constants, and `plan-template.md` quotes them (D13).
4.2 — Invariants: a dry-run note never verifies an item (D1); the prediction rate is computed exactly as before and the rework line is added beside it, never instead (D6, D7); the history is a union and never removes a probe the profile names (D3).
4.3 — Temporal: a note counts only when it stands in the Log before the `status → ready` line — Log order, the same order the evidence rules use (D2); the history is recomputed from the store at every run, so it grows as plans close and never needs editing (D3, D5).
4.4 — Precedence: (1) a backlog plan's pre-verified `pass` line before ready also satisfies D2 for that item — an observed pass is deeper than a dry run; (2) a converged approval is still an approval, so D2 applies to it; (3) D4 asks only probes mapped to items, so a prose answer is never forced into a dry run — the reviewer decides whether the prose is enough; (4) the profile's project probes and the computed set are unioned, neither overrides; (5) the owner decides an exception by recording an `n/a` reason, which the reviewer sees (D1, D2, D3, D4).
4.5 — Every X: "every test and cmd item" is the current `## Baseline` of the plan, parsed by the same `RE.item` the Baseline check uses, so a `file` or `manual` item is excluded by type and an item added after approval by not being in the Baseline (D2); "every done plan" is `loadPlans` over the store directory, the same list the index reads, and a plan file that does not parse is skipped with its existing problem (D3, D8); "every message" is every key of the exported `MESSAGES_CALIBRATION` — all texts D1–D29 introduce — read by D13's case from the object, not from a list typed into the test; "every rule" for D23 is every `calib-mutant` marker in the source, read by the script.

## Design

### Data
3.1 — Inputs and the text each matcher runs over (project probe 3.1): (a) Log lines of a plan, whole file split on `\r?\n`, each line matched whole, ` · ` (space, U+00B7, space) the separator — the dry-run matcher runs only over lines that already matched the `note` grammar, so a line that is not a note is never read as a dry run; the command and printed text may contain `·` only inside backticks, which the matcher does not treat specially, so a note's printed part is everything after the first ` → ` (D1); (b) amendment lines, matched by the extended `RE.amendment`, where `reworks:` must sit in its slot — the same word inside the why is text, not the field (D6); (c) the why text for D9, searched for the substrings `twins:` and `fails when:` anywhere (D9); (d) `profile.md` whole file, sections split on `## ` headings, rows `- …` inside `## Project probes` (probe id first, then ` · `) and `## Host` (D3, D11); (e) note text for D24, matched against the home-path and e-mail shapes `release-check.mjs` uses — Windows backslash, forward-slash and `/Users/` forms (D24). The fixtures spell CRLF and LF files, a `·` inside backticks, and a ` → ` inside a command's code span, which must round-trip (D1, D23).
3.2 — Outputs: problems and warnings through the existing `checkPlan` lists; two printed lines; two index lines; the host summary line; no file is written by the new code except the index through the existing writer (D5, D8, D10, D11).
3.3 — Persistence, temporary files first: `<tmp>/dod-review-*` prompt files and `<tmp>/dod-review-out-cal*` outputs live until this plan closes and are deleted by step 11 (D28); `<tmp>/dod-wbs-browsers` is the Playwright cache the walked checkers share, kept across sessions by wbs-view A6 and outside this child's ownership. Then: dry-run notes live in the plan's Log for the life of the plan, removed only by editing the plan (D1); host rows live in `profile.md`, committed (D12); the index lines are regenerated, never edited (D8); the rehearsal clone `<tmp>/cal-rb` and the mutant copy `<tmp>/cal-mut` are deleted on exit (D21, D23); selftest roots are removed by the existing harness (D20). Only the plan's Log exists once — git is its copy (D20).
3.4 — Migration: none. Old plans need no edit; the index is regenerated once (D8, D15).

### States
7.1 — Empty, first-run, loading, partial and error states. Empty store → `miss history: none yet (0 done plans)` (D5); a plan with no reviews → no D10 line (D10); a profile without the sections → valid (D3, D11); a plan in `draft` is never asked for a dry run until it is approved (D2); a zero-item draft with a malformed dry-run note → exactly the one malformed-note problem and no other (D1). Loading and partial: the script is a synchronous command that prints once when it has read everything, so there is no loading state; a partial read is an error state, which D26 turns into a warning plus a history from what was read (D26).
7.2 — Concurrent use, and which actor is which: the author (an agent, this one included) edits plans and runs `--check`; the session hook runs `--brief`; the owner and the reviewer read. The script takes no lock (the epic ruled out locks). Store-level semantics are retry-on-change: a run that computes the history hashes each file's bytes as it reads it, re-reads and re-hashes every file after the last read (a rewrite that keeps size and modification time is still seen), reads the store once more if any changed, and warns if the second read also changed — so a mixed snapshot is never reported silently (D29). `--check` and the committed index can disagree only when the store changed between the two runs, and that is exactly what `--check-index` and D15's `store-diff` report as a stale index — the regenerated index wins (D8, D15). The index is still written only through `writeAtomic` with its expect-bytes guard. A sibling child (review-loop) edits the same script; this child's build starts only after review-loop closes, and its commits are told apart by touching this plan's file (14.4, D20, D21).
7.3 — Stale data and correction: a dry run is an observation made at approval, and D2 asks for it only there. After approval every item still needs a `pass` dated after its last amendment (evidence rule 4, unchanged), so a `~D<n>` item's old dry run never stands in for fresh evidence — the dry run is not invalidated because it is never used again after approval (D2, D22). A plan reopened after close keeps its notes and is not re-asked, because its approval date does not change (D2).

### Permissions
2.1 — Every actor that can reach it, inherited from the epic's decision that the repository's access control is the policy (dod-v0-2 15.1: no per-user authorization inside the skill): anyone who can write the repository — the owner, an agent in the owner's session, a contributor with push access, a repository admin — can write plans, notes, fields and profile rows; the CI service account (GitHub Actions) runs `npm run validate` and reads; the SessionStart hook runs `--brief` and reads; the reviewer (Codex read-only, a fresh subagent, or the owner) receives the redacted plan including the Log and so sees every dry-run note (review.md, unchanged); an unauthenticated reader of a public copy can read, never write. The authorization boundary is outside dod and is two layers: the operating system's file permissions on the working copy (who can edit files locally — the owner's account and any process it runs, agents included) and the host's push permissions (who can change the shared history); dod runs as whoever invokes it and holds no credential. Whatever any of them writes, the script only reads it: no note, row or line is ever executed, spawned or opened (D25). The owner alone decides approval and each `n/a` exception, which the reviewer sees (D1, D2, D22).
2.2 — The unauthorized path, per write path: a local edit by an account without file permission is refused by the operating system with its error; a push without access is refused by the host; a CI run writes nothing to the repository; the index is written only by `writeAtomic`, which refuses when the file changed under it; a local edit by an authorized account that tampers with an approved plan is refused by nothing and caught after the fact — an edited Baseline line is an existing problem, and a dry-run note back-filled after approval (dated after `baselined`) is D2's problem; git history is the audit record of who wrote what. Inside the script there is nothing to authorize, inside the script there is nothing to authorize, because no input can make it act — a planted command in a note is text, and `calib.inert` fails if it ever runs (D25). An author who skips the dry run meets a problem at approval that names the item and the fix (D2).
2.3 — Ownership: a note belongs to the plan whose Log holds it; a note copied into another plan must name that plan's items or it is a problem (D1); when the author changes mid-plan (a new session, another agent), the notes stay with the plan and the next author is bound by the same rule — ownership is the plan's, not the writer's; a closed plan reopened keeps its notes and is not re-asked (D2); the history belongs to the store, not to any plan (D3).

### UX
11.1 — Discovery: the rule surfaces as a problem at the first `--check` after approval, and SKILL.md's plan step 5 tells the author to dry-run before review (D2, D14).
11.2 — Feedback: every problem names the item and the fix; the history line reads the same whether the store has one done plan or fifty; the host summary says `not set` rather than nothing (D2, D5, D11).
11.3 — Accessibility: plain terminal text, wrapped at 120 columns by the existing `wrapLine`, no colour is the only signal (D5).
11.4 — Activation: the rule activates itself at approval for every plan approved from 2026-10-02; nobody has to ask for it, and the `--check` line shows it ran. The should-not-activate cases are unrelated to approval: a `file` item, a plan approved before the date (D2, D5).

## Security

10.1 — Authorization on every path: the new code's allowed operations are reading files inside the store directory and its `profile.md`, and writing the index through the existing writer; it spawns nothing and passes no option to any program, so there is no option boundary to widen. `calib.inert` spies on every spawn function and on `openSync` while the new code runs over a fixture whose note is a live command, and fails on a spawn, on the command's effect, or on an open outside the store (D25). The two build scripts run only `git`, `node`, `sed` and `bash` inside `<tmp>/cal-rb` and `<tmp>/cal-mut`, each deleted on exit (D21, D23).
10.2 — Injection and unsafe content: note, row and amendment text reaches only the terminal and the generated index, never a shell, query or URL; before it is echoed, C0 and C1 control characters (ANSI escapes) are removed and it is cut at 120 characters by one function (D27); the index lines carry probe ids and numbers only, never note text (D8).
10.3 — Secrets: a dry run prints what a command prints, which on this host includes the home path; the note is warned in draft and a problem from approval on, so it is redacted before the plan is approved (D24), and D18 fails the lab if one is committed. No secret is read or stored by the new code (D18, D24).
10.4 — Personal data: the new code collects none; the only personal data that can enter is what an author pastes into a note, which D24 refuses from approval on (home paths and e-mail addresses); nothing is exported beyond the plan file itself, and git history is the audit trail of who wrote each note. Host rows hold platform and version strings only, never a user or machine name (D12, D18, D24).

## Failure & observability

12.1 — What the author sees: the problem text with the item and the fix (D2, D4, D6); a warning for twins and private output (D9, D24); `✗ host:` lines with exit 1 (D11).
12.2 — What is recorded: the notes themselves are the log of what was observed and when; the report's rework line is the measure (D1, D7).
12.3 — How we would know it is broken, now: the index's two new lines are regenerated at every commit that touches the store and are read at every session start through the index (D8); the signal is the `Rework — done plans` ratio and the per-plan rate of every plan approved from 2026-10-02 — a plan approved under D2 that still closes with discovered misses on a miss-history probe it dry-ran is a finding against this child, and the close report's missed-probes line names it. Beyond this store, the measure is the four machine skills' rates (epic A20; BACKLOG `calibration-measure`) (D5, D8).
12.4 — Failing cases: every rule's case names its planted failure and its silent input (D1–D11, D13, D14, D16, D24–D29); each gating probe has one command able to fail — 2.1 D25 (`calib.inert`), 3.3 D20, 4.4 D2 (`calib.dry-run-at-ready`), 6.2 D26 (`calib.faults`), 10.1 D25, 10.3 D18, 12.4 D23 (`calibration.mutants.sh`), 14.3 D21, 14.4 D20; the empty input never reads as a pass (D5 `none yet`, D10 prints nothing, D11 `not set`, D19 fails on an empty interval — observed in its dry run); fixtures spell the real Log text including CRLF, `·` inside backticks and `→` inside a command, which is the parser state the real run meets (D1, D23).

## Performance

13.1 — The hot path is `--check` over one plan plus a read of every plan's amendments for the history; the index already reads every plan, and the history reuses that list when the index is written. The budget is absolute, not relative: the history over 1,000 generated done plans in under 2,000 ms (`calib.scale`, D3), and the whole selftest under the validator's 180 s per helper (D17).
13.2 — Limits: the history line lists at most 49 probes, every rubric-2 probe, so nothing is cut — `wrapLine` wraps it (D5).
